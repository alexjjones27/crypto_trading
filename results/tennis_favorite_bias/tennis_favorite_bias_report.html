The Favorite Doesn't Pay: Tennis Edition

Same 70%-threshold, walk-forward Kelly methodology as the football version, run on 105,726 ATP and WTA matches (2002–2025) from tennis-data.co.uk — free, no account, same publisher family as football-data.co.uk. Tennis is the cleaner test case: it's a genuine two-outcome market, so "the favorite" maps onto Polymarket's binary YES/NO directly, with no draw to complicate the picture.

Negative, and not close: every threshold, every surface, both decades

Unlike football, this isn't a fragile pooled number that happens to flip under scrutiny — it's uniformly negative. Favorite win rate trails the quoted price at all eight thresholds tested (60% through 95%), on every tour/surface combination, and the split-sample check shows the same roughly −1.2 to −1.3pp edge in 2002–2018 and 2019–2025 alike. Backing tennis favorites at Pinnacle-quoted prices is a structurally losing bet, and quarter-Kelly sizing against a real (if small) negative edge does what Kelly sizing against a negative edge is supposed to do: it wipes the bankroll to $0 at every threshold below 80%.

1. Does the quoted price already predict the outcome?

At every threshold, the favorite's realized win rate sits below the vig-inclusive quoted price — the opposite of a bettor-favorable mispricing. There's no threshold in this range where backing the favorite clears its own break-even bar, let alone the bookmaker's built-in margin on top.

2. Threshold sweep: quarter-Kelly vs. flat 1%-of-equity

Read the "final equity" columns literally: at every threshold from 60% to 75%, quarter-Kelly sizing against this small persistent negative edge doesn't just underperform — it ends at $0.00. Flat staking loses far less because it doesn't compound the same losing edge into ever-larger position sizes as the walk-forward belief (correctly) turns pessimistic.

4. Split-sample robustness
