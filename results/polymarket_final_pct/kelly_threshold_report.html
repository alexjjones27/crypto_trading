The entry bar isn't a risk dial

Raw return, risk-adjusted return, and trade count, side by side across five thresholds. None of the three peaks in the same place.

0.980 wins on raw return. 0.990 wins on Sharpe. 0.999 doesn't win at all — it wins nothing, because almost nothing clears three consecutive snapshots at 99.9% and survives the exact-score/weather exclusion: 11 trades in the whole sample, versus 2,000–2,100 at every other threshold. A tighter entry bar reads as "safer," but past a certain point it just means the strategy stops having enough trades to exist, which is a different failure mode than the one the bar is supposed to guard against.

Same strategy, same threshold, same day-to-day methodology — Sharpe moves from 2.24 to 4.09 on sampling alone. Nothing about the underlying edge changed; a different stratified draw from a population that grows every day landed on a different handful of flips. Read every number in this report, and in Kelly, Capped, the same way: as one realized path through a resampling distribution that is still wide, not as a precise coefficient. The threshold ranking above (0.98 > 0.99 > 0.995 by return; 0.99 > 0.995 > 0.98 by Sharpe) should be read as directionally real — loosening the bar increases both return and trade count, and 0.999 is a hard floor on liquidity — but not as a precise optimum to the third decimal.

Limitations specific to this sweep

  • Five thresholds, five samples that mostly — but not perfectly — overlap. Each threshold's 4,016-market sample was redrawn from the live census rather than filtered from one fixed pool, since the crossing signal itself is threshold-dependent. The $0.990 vs. $0.990 (original sample) comparison above is the direct measurement of how much that redraw alone moves the answer.
  • 0.999's zero-flip result is not evidence the strategy is safer there. Eleven trades cannot support any claim about tail risk in either direction; it is absence of a sample, not presence of safety.
  • Kelly sizing at each threshold uses that threshold's own walk-forward flip-rate belief (same Beta(1,300) prior, same per-category shrinkage as Kelly, Capped) — the position sizes are not held constant across thresholds, so return differences reflect both the raw edge and how confidently that edge got sized.
