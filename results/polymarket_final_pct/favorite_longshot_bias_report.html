Short the longshot. Never buy it.

Every trade in this backtest population sits at a market's extreme: one side priced near $1, the other near $0. Betting against the near‑certain longshot (buying the ~99%+ complement) and betting on it (buying the ~1%‑or‑under token directly) are exact mirror images of the same 3,364‑trade population. Tested both, at flat position sizing, across the full 70–99.9% probability range — and audited the sampling pipeline for the bias/data‑mining risk any result this clean should raise.

Buying the longshot needed roughly 1 win in 212 to break even; it realized 1 in 1,032 — three wins total, all genuine tail events (a surprise SNL cameo, a fast crypto spike, an unusual earthquake cluster). Under any compounding position size the strategy is ruinous: 586 sequential flat‑1% bets compound losses to zero before the next win arrives. This is favorite‑longshot bias, directly measured rather than assumed.

Bug found & fixed

A direct check turned up a real reproducibility bug, not just reassurance: a first attempt at the threshold sweep above shared only ~1% of its sampled markets with a batch run a day earlier, despite both using the documented fixed seed. Root cause was two compounding issues in stratified_sample_markets — a non‑deterministic dedup order, and one random-number stream shared sequentially across every market category, so a change to any single category's size (new markets resolving elsewhere) silently desynced every other category's draw.

Fixed with a stable sort before sampling and a per‑category deterministic seed with shuffle‑ then‑slice selection, verified with a synthetic before/after‑growth test and a permanent regression test. The chart and table above are from the corrected, single‑run sweep. This does not affect the mirror‑trade comparison, the core Final‑1% dataset, or any other result built on it earlier in this project — each of those is one internally self‑consistent draw; only cross‑run comparability was broken.

Other checks run:

No cherry‑picking. All 10 thresholds tested are reported above; none were dropped.
No look‑ahead. The crossing signal only ever sees past prices; nothing about future resolution leaks into entry timing.
Non‑independence, acknowledged. The threshold buckets overlap — a market that reaches 99% typically passed through 90% and 95% on the way — so treat this as one coherent picture across a spectrum, not ten independent confirmations.
Small‑sample fragility, acknowledged. The tightest thresholds (99%+) rest on 0–4 flips; their exact dollar figures carry real estimation error even though the qualitative pattern (flip rate falling smoothly toward zero) is well supported by the hundreds of flips at looser thresholds.

Reading this correctly. This is a historical backtest on resolved Polymarket markets, not a live track record. Fill prices are the last observed CLOB snapshot before entry, not a guaranteed executable quote — the same limitation as every other backtest in this project.
