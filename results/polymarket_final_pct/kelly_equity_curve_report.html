The drawdown

The entire 33.25% max drawdown is one trade: “Will Kamala Harris go on SNL?” (No), resolved Nov 3, 2024 — she made a surprise cameo the Saturday before the election. With no cap, full‑Kelly sizing put $3,721.40, 33.25% of the whole portfolio, on that single position; losing it dropped equity from $11,193 to $7,472 in one day. Find it on the chart below.

What removing the cap actually bought

Same edge, same 3,364‑trade sample — only the exposure ceiling changes. Uncapped sizing roughly triples total return, but concentrates capital into far fewer trades: 1,480 later signals get skipped for lack of free capital (vs. 24 under the 65% cap), because early Kelly‑sized bets tie up most of the bankroll before later opportunities arrive.

The Sharpe ratio inverts the return ranking (2.33 capped vs. 1.34 uncapped) because uncapped sizing makes the outcome hinge on a small number of large, concentrated bets rather than spreading the edge across the full trade set. The single SNL flip proves the mechanism: identical edge, identical mistake, but a 100× larger dollar cost purely from position sizing. How much of this is one lucky path

Uncapped sizing makes the outcome far more sensitive to exactly which flip lands and when, so the capped backtest's bootstrap doesn't transfer here. This is a fresh 1,000‑iteration moving block bootstrap — resampling calendar‑month blocks of trades with replacement — run directly against the uncapped sizing.

The realized $21,899 path did better than the bootstrap median of $16,827 — the actual sequence of flips happened to land favorably. Max drawdown's own median across resamples is 62.7% (95th percentile: 92.65%), because with no ceiling a single unlucky flip at high equity can cost a third or more of the book, and some resamples stack more than one. This is the real trade‑off for the uncapped route's higher point‑estimate return: a wide realistic range that includes losing money, not just a smaller expected gain.

Reading this correctly. This is a historical backtest on resolved Polymarket markets, not a live track record — no trades were placed. Returns assume maker fills (resting limit orders, $0 fee + category rebate) and fill sizes capped by realized nearby trade volume as a liquidity proxy, since historical order‑book depth isn’t recoverable for resolved markets. Uncapped Kelly sizing is a textbook case of estimation‑error sensitivity: the flip‑rate belief driving position size rests on a handful of observed flips per category, and full Kelly assumes that belief is exact. The capped configuration exists specifically to bound the cost of that belief being wrong.
