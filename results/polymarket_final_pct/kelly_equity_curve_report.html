Two companion strategies

Final-1% is a probabilistic edge -- it bets on a flip-rate belief, and can lose. The two strategies below are structurally different, run alongside it rather than as variants of it. One is a genuine, rigorously backtestable arbitrage; the other -- market-making -- turned out not to be honestly backtestable at all with the data this project has access to, which is itself the answer worth recording.

NegRisk basket arbitrage

Polymarket's NegRisk markets group N mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive outcomes (e.g. "who wins the election") under one basket, with an explicit "Other" catch-all leg guaranteeing the set is complete. Whenever the legs' fee-adjusted prices sum to under $1, buying one share of every leg costs less than $1 and redeems for exactly $1 via CTF merge regardless of which outcome wins -- a completeness arbitrage, not a directional bet.

Not a real backtest

Polymarket's /book endpoint 404s on resolved markets, so there is no historical order-book depth to determine what price a resting quote would actually have been filled at, or how often. What follows is an explicitly toy, parameterized estimate built from trade prints alone -- a sensitivity grid, not a point estimate, because every input is an assumption rather than a measurement.

Market-making, stylized proxy

Pure spread capture: a resting quote is assumed to capture a fixed share of each real trade's size, earning exactly the assumed half-spread per share, with the spread capped at 30% of the distance to $0 or $1 so it stays sane at the extreme prices this market population is full of. No inventory-direction term -- two earlier versions of this model (mark to resolution; mark to the next trade print) were tried and rejected because both leaked spurious directional profit unrelated to market-making. See the panel note below for what that leaves out.

Why this isn't shown as a return on $10,000. A market maker's P&L scales with how much order flow it touches across many markets running at once, not with a single bankroll compounding trade-to-trade the way Final-1% or the basket arb do. Framing this as "+1,300% on $10k" would imply a bankroll-bounded strategy it isn't; the chart below is cumulative dollar P&L from operating capped ($25/trade) quotes across the whole market population, which is the honest unit for what this model actually represents.

What "not modeled" costs. Real market makers lose money precisely when informed flow moves the market against inventory they haven't unwound yet -- adverse selection. This model assumes every captured position flattens instantly and costlessly at the assumed spread, which is why it's presented as a best case rather than an expected one. The two more elaborate models that tried to include a directional term were rejected (see the script's docstring) because both leaked unrelated profit into the total rather than genuinely capturing adverse selection risk -- getting that right needs real order-book data this project doesn't have, not a better guess.

Reading this correctly. This is a historical backtest on resolved Polymarket markets, not a live track record — no trades were placed. Returns assume maker fills (resting limit orders, $0 fee + category rebate) and fill sizes capped by realized nearby trade volume as a liquidity proxy, since historical order‑book depth isn’t recoverable for resolved markets. Uncapped Kelly sizing is a textbook case of estimation‑error sensitivity: the flip‑rate belief driving position size rests on a handful of observed flips per category, and full Kelly assumes that belief is exact. The capped configuration exists specifically to bound the cost of that belief being wrong.
