Why fewer trades get taken uncapped: with no ceiling, each Kelly‑sized bet compounds against a shrinking pool of "room" as positions concentrate — capital gets committed early and heavily to whichever markets cross first, so 1,480 later signals get skipped for lack of free capital, versus only 24 under the 65% cap. The capped version's near‑10× lower drawdown and higher Sharpe come from spreading the same edge across far more of the available trades rather than betting big on fewer of them.

How much of this is one lucky path

Every number above is from the single historical sequence of trades that actually happened. A 1,000‑iteration moving block bootstrap — resampling calendar‑month blocks of trades with replacement — gives a distribution instead of one point. (Run at the 50%‑cap config used before the exposure‑cap sweep; the 65%‑cap curve above is not expected to shift this picture materially, since the cap mainly reduces capital‑skipped trades rather than changing which trades exist.)

Reading this correctly. This is a historical backtest on resolved Polymarket markets, not a live track record — no trades were placed. Returns assume maker fills (resting limit orders, $0 fee + category rebate) and fill sizes capped by realized nearby trade volume as a liquidity proxy, since historical order‑book depth isn’t recoverable for resolved markets. The edge itself rests on a 3‑flip sample across 2,367 qualifying trades; see the flip‑rate confidence interval analysis in the base report for how wide that uncertainty really is.
