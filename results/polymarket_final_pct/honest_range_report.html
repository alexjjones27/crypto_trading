The Honest Range

Every number in this series so far came from one realized sequence of trades. This resamples that sequence 1,000 times and re-derives the two claims this whole strategy leans on hardest — and revises one of them down.

1,000 block-bootstrap resamples 36 monthly blocks resampled with replacement exclusion rule tested on a time-split held-out half
01

The headline number was on the lucky side

Resample calendar-month blocks of trades with replacement, stitch them back-to-back preserving each block's internal order, re-run the identical walk-forward Kelly sim. 1,000 times. The historical path we reported sits above the median of what that same underlying data plausibly produces.

Reported CAGR: 6.6%. Bootstrap median: 3.6%. The number in every table so far was roughly the 70th percentile outcome, not the typical one.

The reassuring part: 0 of 1,000 resamples lost money, and the 5th-percentile CAGR is still +1.6%. The edge itself looks real and durable across resamples — it's the size of the edge that the single historical path overstated, not its existence.

The part worth sitting with: the Sharpe ratio's 5th-to-95th percentile range is 0.71 to 4.89 — a roughly 7× spread from the same underlying trades, just resampled differently. Every Sharpe number quoted anywhere in this report series, including 2.24, should be read as one draw from that range, not a precise coefficient.

Does the exclusion rule survive its own blind spot?

The exact-score/weather exclusion was built by reading all 7 flips in the full sample — in-sample by construction. Split the sample at its time median and re-measure the rule's effect in each half independently, as if the second half had never been seen when the rule was written.

Early half

The rule holds in both halves, not just one. A rule that only "worked" because of one lucky/unlucky cluster would show a strong effect in one half and roughly nothing in the other. Both halves show a real, positive reduction — weaker early, much stronger late.

But notice what else moved: the exact-score/weather trade count itself grew from 47 (across 3 years) to 222 (across 6 months) — roughly 15× the density. Both of the exact-score flips, and one of the two weather flips, only exist in the late half because the market type barely existed at volume before then. That's consistent with two different stories this data can't fully separate: a genuinely emerging, structurally riskier market category as Polymarket's live/exact-value product surface has grown, or a rule that's still mostly resting on a handful of events in a newly-dense corner of the sample. Both are true enough to act on; neither is proven at this n.

What actually changes

Nothing here reverses a conclusion — it recalibrates confidence in one and strengthens the other. The exclusion rule now has real (if still thin) out-of-sample support: it wasn't just explaining the data it was fit on. The headline Kelly return, on the other hand, should be quoted as a range, not a point: call it roughly 2–6% CAGR, with 6.6% at the upper end of what a fair resampling of the same 3.5 years supports — and Sharpe anywhere from "mediocre" to "excellent" depending on which months get more or less weight, which is exactly the kind of statement the sample-size limitation in every prior report was gesturing at without a number attached. Now it has one.
